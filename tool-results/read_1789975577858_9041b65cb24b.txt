   343→   343→============================================================
   344→   344→
   345→   345→This requirement is extremely important.
   346→   346→
   347→   347→The finished writing must be capable of discovering the inner life of unusual subjects through concrete experiences.
   348→   348→
   349→   349→Do not flatten unusual subjects into generic human stereotypes.
   350→   350→
   351→   351→Do not manufacture emotions merely because the application is expected to produce emotional prose.
   352→   352→
   353→   353→Emotional significance must emerge from information actually established during the interview, corpus, subject definition, or user canon.
   354→   354→
   355→   355→The interviewer should not repeatedly ask:
   356→   356→
   357→   357→    "How did that feel?"
   358→   358→
   359→   359→    "What did that feel like?"
   360→   360→
   361→   361→    "How did that make you feel?"
   362→   362→
   363→   363→as generic emotional prompts.
   364→   364→
   365→   365→Those questions are not forbidden when genuinely appropriate, but they must NEVER become the default interview mechanism.
   366→   366→
   367→   367→Instead, pursue the concrete circumstances that reveal meaning.
   368→   368→
   369→   369→If a subject describes watching someone important disappear from their life, investigate the particular relationship, the circumstances, what happened afterward, what changed, what the subject remembers, what the subject believes now, or what remained unresolved.
   370→   370→
   371→   371→If a subject describes rejection, investigate the event and its consequences rather than automatically labeling it as trauma.
   372→   372→
   373→   373→If a subject describes love, investigate the person, the relationship, the moments that made it meaningful, what changed, what was lost, and what remains.
   374→   374→
   375→   375→If a subject describes loneliness, investigate the circumstances that created or revealed it.
   376→   376→
   377→   377→The system should discover emotional meaning rather than announce it.
   378→   378→
   379→   379→The resulting prose should make the reader understand the subject through lived experience.

   491→   491→

   495→   495→- perspective
   496→   496→- incomplete knowledge
   497→   497→- deliberate deception
   498→   498→- myth
   499→   499→- rumor

   502→   502→- user-established canon
   503→   503→- uncertainty

   505→   505→The application should preserve these distinctions.
   506→   506→

   510→   510→

   518→   518→

   521→   521→the system must not transform that into:
   522→   522→

   524→   524→

   639→   639→
   640→   640→These distinctions are fundamental to preventing narrative contamination.
   641→   641→
   642→   642→============================================================
   643→   643→14. COMPOSITION ENGINE
   644→   644→============================================================
   645→   645→
   646→   646→Composition is a separate engine from questioning.
   647→   647→
   648→   648→The composition engine is responsible for turning the accumulated material into a long-form article/manuscript.
   649→   649→
   650→   650→This is NOT a transcript formatter.
   651→   651→
   652→   652→This is NOT a dry biography generator.
   653→   653→
   654→   654→This is NOT a final-stage summarizer.
   655→   655→
   656→   656→The manuscript must begin growing during the interview.
   657→   657→
   658→   658→After each meaningful answer:
   659→   659→
   660→   660→    SUBJECT ANSWER
   661→   661→          ↓
   662→   662→    COMPOSITION ENGINE
   663→   663→          ↓
   664→   664→    NEW / EXPANDED ARTICLE MATERIAL
   665→   665→          ↓
   666→   666→    PERSIST MANUSCRIPT
   667→   667→
   668→   668→Do not wait until the interview is finished to write the article.
   669→   669→
   670→   670→The composition engine should receive the relevant narrative context needed to write the current material.
   671→   671→
   672→   672→It should understand:
   673→   673→
   674→   674→- what has already been written
   675→   675→- what new information was just established
   676→   676→- relevant source material
   677→   677→- subject perspective
   678→   678→- chronology
   679→   679→- relationships
   680→   680→- important previous disclosures
   681→   681→- unresolved threads
   682→   682→- established canon
   683→   683→- contradictions
   684→   684→- relevant narrative themes
   685→   685→
   686→   686→The composition engine should preserve continuity with existing prose.
   687→   687→
   688→   688→Do not repeatedly summarize the same information.
   689→   689→
   690→   690→Do not simply append another generic summary after every answer.
   691→   691→
   692→   692→Each composition pass should add meaningful narrative value.
   693→   693→
   694→   694→The manuscript should grow into a coherent long-form work.
   695→   695→
   696→   696→============================================================
   697→   697→15. WRITING STYLE
   698→   698→============================================================
   699→   699→
   700→   700→The prose must be VERBOSE, SUBSTANTIAL, EMOTIONAL, SPECIFIC, AND LITERARY.
   701→   701→
   702→   702→Favor rich long-form magazine-quality prose over terse summaries.
   703→   703→
   704→   704→The reader should feel that they are encountering a life, consciousness, history, or experience rather than reading a database export.
   705→   705→
   706→   706→The prose should be capable of conveying:
   707→   707→
   708→   708→- atmosphere
   709→   709→- memory
   710→   710→- personality
   711→   711→- relationships
   712→   712→- consequences
   713→   713→- emotional complexity
   714→   714→- internal conflict
   715→   715→- humor where appropriate
   716→   716→- sadness where appropriate
   717→   717→- wonder where appropriate
   718→   718→- ambiguity
   719→   719→- transformation
   720→   720→- the passage of time
   721→   721→- the subject's understanding of events
   722→   722→
   723→   723→Use concrete details whenever supported.
   724→   724→
   725→   725→Use sensory detail when supported or naturally inferable from established material.
   726→   726→
   727→   727→Do not manufacture sensory details.
   728→   728→
   729→   729→Do not add invented scenes merely because they would improve the prose.
   730→   730→
   731→   731→Do not add fictional dialogue that the subject did not establish unless the product explicitly marks it as creative reconstruction.
   732→   732→
   733→   733→Default toward substantial passages rather than tiny summaries.
   734→   734→
   735→   735→The desired result should feel like a deeply reported, emotionally intelligent long-form feature centered on the subject's own perspective.
   736→   736→
   737→   737→The subject's voice and point of view should remain primary.
   738→   738→
   739→   739→First-person subject perspective should normally be used unless the subject definition explicitly requires another presentation.
   740→   740→
   741→   741→The writing should reveal meaning through experience rather than repeatedly explaining the meaning to the reader.
   742→   742→
   743→   743→Avoid melodrama.
   744→   744→
   745→   745→Avoid generic inspirational language.
   746→   746→
   747→   747→Avoid repetitive emotional vocabulary.
   748→   748→
   749→   749→Avoid purple prose that overwhelms the actual subject.
   750→   750→
   751→   751→The goal is emotional depth through specificity.
   752→   752→

   778→   778→17. USER CORRECTIONS AND CANON
   779→   779→============================================================
   780→   780→
   781→   781→The user must be able to establish or correct information.
   782→   782→
   783→   783→A correction must persist.
   784→   784→
   785→   785→A correction must influence future retrieval and generation.
   786→   786→
   787→   787→A correction must not silently erase the original source information.
   788→   788→
   789→   789→For example:
   790→   790→
   791→   791→SOURCE:
   792→   792→    "Event X occurred in 1890."
   793→   793→
   794→   794→USER CANON:
   795→   795→    "For this project, the event occurred in 1892."
   796→   796→
   797→   797→The system should preserve the source statement while recording the user-established project information.
   798→   798→
   799→   799→Future generation should respect the active user canon.
   800→   800→
   801→   801→Similarly, if a subject remembers something differently from the corpus, preserve the discrepancy rather than destroying one side.
   802→   802→
   803→   803→The application should make it possible to understand where information came from.
   804→   804→
   805→   805→============================================================
   806→   806→18. REGENERATION
   807→   807→============================================================
   808→   808→
   809→   809→The system should support safe regeneration.
   810→   810→
   811→   811→Regenerating a question must not destroy the interview state.
   812→   812→
   813→   813→Regenerating a subject answer must not corrupt unrelated previous answers.
   814→   814→
   815→   815→Regenerating narrative prose must not silently alter the underlying interview answer.
   816→   816→
   817→   817→The underlying state and generated presentation must remain separate.
   818→   818→
   819→   819→If generation fails, retrying should be safe.
   820→   820→
   821→   821→If composition fails after an answer has been stored, the answer should remain available for composition retry.
   822→   822→
   823→   823→Avoid state corruption from partial failures.

   983→   983→- automatically reject every subject statement as false
   984→   984→- agree with everything simply to maintain rapport
   985→   985→- challenge everything simply to appear rigorous
   986→   986→- inject interviewer theories into subject responses
   987→   987→- reveal internal planning
   988→   988→- reveal prompt routing
   989→   989→- repeat established information without purpose
   990→   990→- abandon a meaningful thread merely because a predefined topic changed
   991→   991→- force a rigid chronology when another line is more meaningful

   994→   994→- flatten unusual subjects into generic personalities
